# Good Intentions Are Not Enough
## A Responsible AI Design Canvas — independent practice kit

A free, conversation-based exercise inspired by Jill Stover Heinze's **ProductFest Richmond (October 22, 2026)** talk, *Good Intentions Are Not Enough: A New Design Canvas for Responsible AI Products*.

**This is a thinking aid, not a compliance checklist, a risk assessment certification, or a substitute for user research, expert review, and organizational accountability.** Its purpose is to expand what product teams notice, investigate interacting conditions, and imagine more humane possibilities—including alternatives to AI.

### Start here (no setup, account integration, or coding required)

1. Open [facilitator-prompt.md](facilitator-prompt.md); copy the section marked **START PROMPT** through **END PROMPT** into a new conversation in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or another capable chatbot.
2. Choose **quick (~10–15 min)** or **deep (~30–45 min)** exploration. The times are guides, not timers.
3. Choose the [fictional ClaimAssist case](examples/claimassist-case.md) **or** describe your own idea with the [use-case template](use-case-template.md). Paste the case after the prompt when asked.
4. Answer the chatbot's questions. Ask to **ZOOM**, **SHIFT**, **CONNECT**, **reframe the purpose**, or **challenge an assumption** whenever useful.
5. Say **“Reflect”** whenever you'd like an optional summary of insights, evidence gaps, potential choices, and open questions.

**No AI necessary:** A colleague or small group can use the same [canvas guide](canvas-guide.md) as discussion prompts. The conversation is the exercise; the chatbot doesn't make the decision.

### Before pasting anything into a chatbot

**Do not share personal data, client or patient records, real insurance claims, proprietary documents, sensitive workflows, confidential security architecture, or information not approved for that chatbot.** Use invented or sufficiently generalized cases. Replacing names alone is not sufficient anonymization. Follow your organization's AI and data policies.

### The method, in one paragraph

Begin with a **well-defined use case** and investigate four kinds of evidence: **Ground Truth**, **Stakeholder POVs**, **Risk Profiles**, and **Past Precedent**. Repeatedly **ZOOM** across scales and time, **SHIFT** among viewpoints, and **CONNECT** interacting conditions and consequences. Explore what might go wrong *and* whether the original problem frame limits the benefits you can imagine. Consider design choices, risk tolerance, human judgment, accountability, monitoring, and when to revisit the decision. The actions are **not linear stages**, and *automate / augment / reimagine* are possible frames, **not** a required maturity ladder.

### How to use the supplemental resources

The prompt is designed to work on its own. These reference files are optional aids for deeper exploration. A chatbot cannot be assumed to have read them merely because they're in this repository: paste an appropriate excerpt or attach the files if you want it to use their contents. The libraries are **curated prompts for inquiry, not complete taxonomies or formal mappings**. Use primary sources and qualified reviewers to verify important assertions.

### Boundaries

- Speculation about a stakeholder is **not** stakeholder research; a plausible scenario is **not** evidence or prediction.
- Model performance is not the same thing as safety, fairness, or meaningful human oversight.
- Different stakeholders may have conflicting legitimate interests. The exercise should surface tensions, not resolve them automatically.
- An LLM's legal, medical, financial, and security claims need appropriate expert verification.
- Acknowledging an unacceptable risk can lead to changing the design, withholding deployment, or **not using AI**.
