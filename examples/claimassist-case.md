# ClaimAssist at Harbor Mutual — fictional participant case

> **Entirely fictional teaching scenario.** Harbor Mutual, ClaimAssist, the operational details, pilot outcomes, and people described here are invented. This is not a vendor description, empirical study, or forecast. Do not confuse this home-insurance example with actual health-insurance litigation.

## The decision

Harbor Mutual is a regional property insurer. Leaders want to settle homeowners' water-damage claims faster without increasing headcount. A pilot of **ClaimAssist** looks encouraging; leaders are considering a wider January rollout to more adjusters. **Would you support rollout as proposed? What else would you want to know?**

## What the product does

ClaimAssist is a **generative-AI decision-support system**, not an autonomous claims agent. It examines claim descriptions and uploaded photographs, retrieves relevant policy language, and **drafts** a coverage recommendation, settlement estimate, and customer letter. A human **claims adjuster** (the staff member who assesses damage, policy coverage, and recommended payment) may approve, edit, or escalate the draft. Existing operational systems issue the letter and payment **only after human authorization**. There is no separate claims chatbot in this case.

## Pilot snapshot — invented numbers

- Average time to settle a claim: **19 days → 6 days**.
- Adjusters agreed with AI recommendations **94%** of the time.
- Customer satisfaction rose **11 points**.
- The pilot involved **1,200 water-damage claims and 10 experienced adjusters**, during comparatively calm claim volumes.

These are encouraging product indicators. But **agreement is not verified correctness**, and a limited pilot may not represent all conditions.

## Additional contextual clues

- Experienced pilot adjusters sometimes **called claimants informally** to check unusual details before approving the AI draft. That activity wasn't part of the standardized proposed workflow.
- Photographs can miss concealed water damage (for example, damage behind a wall).
- Claimants can be displaced, under time pressure, unfamiliar with policy language, and uncertain how to challenge an estimate.
- During expansion, planned caseloads could rise from approximately **120 to 300 claims per adjuster**. The performance dashboard rewards faster completion, while formal escalation can add **about five days**. Less-experienced adjusters will join the workflow.
- Prior company review flagged **regional differences in historical settlement practices**. Those differences might or might not translate into unjust outcomes; further investigation is needed.
- Storms can sharply increase claims. The pilot has not established how review quality holds up under those conditions.
