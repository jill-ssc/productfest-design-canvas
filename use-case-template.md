# Responsible AI Design Canvas — use-case starter

Use this short template to help a chatbot understand the product or proposal you want to explore. **You don't need complete answers.** Unknowns are legitimate starting points. Paste your completed version **after the facilitator prompt**, or give the information conversationally.

> **Do not paste sensitive information into an unapproved chatbot.** Omit names, identifiers, personal information, actual claim or patient details, confidential workflows, proprietary data, security architecture, customer documents, and anything your organization has not approved for sharing. Generalize, anonymize carefully, or invent a comparable fictional case. Removing names alone may not make information safe.

## Copy and fill in

**Working title:** [e.g., an AI-assisted service workflow]

**The human problem or opportunity:** [What needs improving for people, not only the efficiency target? What outcome would matter to them?]

**Proposed AI role:** [What would AI do? Generate, summarize, recommend, classify, retrieve, interact, take action?]

**Current or intended workflow:** [At a high level, what happens before and after the AI step?]

**Human oversight and system autonomy:** [Who reviews, decides, acts, appeals, or intervenes? What could the system do without asking? Unknown is okay.]

**People and groups affected:** [Users, staff, customers, bystanders, people bearing downstream effects; describe groups, not individuals.]

**Operating context:** [Relevant environment, volume or workload patterns, organizational incentives, constraints, relevant standards or obligations if known. Avoid confidential detail.]

**Evidence we already have:** [Observations, pilot results, complaints, studies, or actual stakeholder research; note their limits.]

**Important unknowns or concerns:** [What don't we know? Where could our present framing be too narrow?]

**The choice or question I want to explore:** [e.g., where AI belongs, whether to launch, what to test, whose needs are overlooked, or whether to reframe the problem entirely.]

**Exploration depth:** [Quick, ~10–15 minutes / Deep, ~30–45 minutes / Unsure]

## Optional worked starting point (fictional, simplified)

**Working title:** ClaimAssist, at fictional insurer Harbor Mutual.

**The human problem or opportunity:** Help households recover from insured property damage while addressing delays and adjuster workload.

**Proposed AI role:** Generative AI analyzes claim descriptions and photos, retrieves policy language, and drafts a recommendation, estimated settlement, and letter for human review.

**Current or intended workflow:** An adjuster approves, edits, or escalates drafts; existing systems issue decisions and payments only after human authorization. The AI is **decision support**, not an autonomous claims-paying agent.

**People and groups affected:** Claimants, new and experienced adjusters, managers, and other stakeholders affected by claim quality and timeliness.

**Evidence we already have:** Fictional pilot results suggest settlement time decreased from 19 to 6 days, 94% agreement between adjusters and AI recommendations, and an 11-point improvement in customer satisfaction. **Agreement does not establish correctness.** Experienced adjusters also made informal claimant calls.

**Important unknowns:** Whether those checks continue at higher workloads; how concealed damage is handled; how incentives, escalation time, and experience shape meaningful review.

**The choice to explore:** Whether and how Harbor should expand the system, and whether the original efficiency-centered product framing should change.

*All ClaimAssist facts and metrics above are part of an invented teaching scenario, not empirical findings.*

## A few tips

- **Begin with the human purpose, not just the technology.** “Reduce call time” and “help people resolve problems confidently” may lead to different design possibilities.
- **Be candid about unknowns.** A useful question is often more valuable than a made-up answer.
- **You are free to challenge the premise.** The exercise can consider redesigning the workflow, limiting AI's role, or not using AI at all.
- **The chatbot cannot validate real stakeholder experiences by imagining them.** Confirm hypotheses with appropriate research, subject-matter experts, tests, and organizational decision-makers.
