# Sources and further reading

**Checked October 21, 2026.** These are primary sources when possible. The toolkit offers an accessible interpretation of their ideas and **does not claim formal compliance, endorsement, or complete coverage**. Reference versions and official guidance can change.

## Risk management and evaluation

1. **NIST (2023).** *Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)*, NIST AI 100-1. https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.100-1 — Voluntary, use-case-agnostic framework. **Govern, Map, Measure, Manage** and the trustworthy AI characteristics. Its functions are not a linear checklist.
2. **NIST (2024).** *Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile*, NIST AI 600-1. https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1 — Companion framework for risks and considerations particular to generative AI.
3. **NIST (2026).** *ARIA Evaluation Planning Manual: Elements of ARIA-Style AI Evaluations*, NIST AI 200-3. https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.200-3 — Planning evaluations that combine model testing, red teaming, and user testing. Helpful for turning inquiry into questions that can be investigated.
4. **NIST AI RMF Playbook.** https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework/nist-ai-rmf-playbook — Companion suggestions for putting the RMF into practice.

## LLM and agentic security

5. **OWASP GenAI Security Project (2026).** *OWASP GenAI LLM Top 10 2026*. https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-genai-llm-top-10-2026/ — Security-focused application risks. Use alongside, not as a replacement for, social and organizational inquiry.
6. **OWASP GenAI Security Project (2026).** *OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications for 2026*. https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-top-10-for-agentic-applications-for-2026/ — Threats for systems with substantial tool use, planning and action authority; not all apply to simple drafting assistants.

## Organizations, human error and moral imagination

7. **Reason, J. (2000).** *Human error: models and management.* BMJ, 320, 768–770. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.320.7237.768 — System versus person approaches; active failures, latent conditions, and layers of defense.
8. **Moral imagination source — bibliographic confirmation pending.** The toolkit's interpretation draws on an excerpt supplied by the presenter describing how institutional narratives and narrow mental models can prevent managers from envisioning moral consequences and alternatives. **Do not attribute to a named author or reproduce the photograph without verifying the book title, edition, page and republication rights.** The concept is used here as a paraphrased inspiration, not as a quoted or fully cited textual source.

## Important distinction

- A **source** provides research or a framework; a **toolkit prompt** uses it to pose questions.
- An **analogy** invites investigation; it doesn't substantiate the same outcome in another industry.
- A **claim in litigation** isn't a court finding or a general population statistic.
- **ClaimAssist** is a constructed scenario and provides no empirical evidence of real insurance claims.
