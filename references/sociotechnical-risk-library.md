# Sociotechnical risks and opportunities — inquiry cards

**Purpose:** Prompt investigation into harms and opportunities that emerge from *systems of technology, people, organizations and institutions*. These are **potential mechanisms**, not assertions about a specific product or a comprehensive academic taxonomy.

| Possible condition | What can interact | Useful question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Happy-path fixation | Pilot success, edge cases, seasonal pressure | Which users and conditions are missing from our success story? |
| Narrow problem framing | Efficiency KPI, existing process, status quo interests | Whose human goal are we actually optimizing? What else could the product be? |
| Automation bias / overreliance | Confident outputs, weak independent checks, reviewer fatigue | Can a person realistically detect and disagree with mistakes? |
| Misaligned incentives | Speed rewards, high workload, delay penalties | Do operating targets make the safer choice harder? |
| Paper-only human oversight | Approval click, lack of context or authority | What conditions make human review meaningful in practice? |
| Unequal ability to benefit or contest | Language, accessibility, time, money, digital access | Who finds the system hardest to use or challenge, and how will we learn? |
| Historical pattern reproduction | Legacy decisions, datasets, rules | What prior practices might the system reproduce or amplify? |
| Invisible externalities | Cost transferred to customers, communities, staff | Who pays for a gain recorded elsewhere as productivity? |
| Diffuse ownership | Vendors, product, operations, model team | Who is accountable at the junction between model behavior and human outcomes? |
| Missing feedback / recourse | Hidden dissatisfaction, low complaint rates, inaccessible appeal | What harms remain unseen because people cannot or do not report them? |
| Scale and cumulative effects | Wider rollout, competitors' imitation, time | What changes when a local practice becomes an institutional norm? |
| Erosion of skill or autonomy | Reliance on generated judgment, deskilling, surveillance | What human capabilities and choices are expanded or reduced? |
| Underexamined possibility | “We must automate this task” | What would become possible if we started with human well-being instead? |

## Apply ZOOM / SHIFT / CONNECT

- **ZOOM:** Ask how an apparent improvement for one task scales to teams, industry practices, and longer-term effects.
- **SHIFT:** Ask whose needs, knowledge, bargaining power, or recourse change the interpretation of success.
- **CONNECT:** Trace a plausible mechanism, such as workload increase → pressure for rapid approval → weak scrutiny → overlooked damage → difficult appeal. Label every unsupported link a **hypothesis**.

## Evidence discipline

Don't put words in stakeholder mouths. An invented persona can highlight questions but cannot establish lived experience. Prefer direct research, observation, domain expertise, independent outcome evaluation, and verified precedents before treating an idea as fact.

**Background:** [James Reason, *Human error: models and management* (2000)](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.320.7237.768); [NIST AI RMF 1.0](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.100-1); [NIST GenAI Profile](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1). The application to these inquiry cards is this toolkit's synthesis, not language quoted from the sources.
